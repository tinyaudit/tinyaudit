TinyAudit · post-review experiments

The parity illusion is real, but not where the draft says it is

The review asked whether compression causes the fairness collapse, or whether any equally broken model would do the same. The control answers it: compression is specific, and the mechanisms leave opposite signatures. One earlier claim on this page has been retracted, and the retraction is itself a finding.

10 seeds · 80 cells Adult + COMPAS 275 tests, 1 pre-existing failure all numbers from committed CSVs

Two corrections first

Both came out of the regenerated sweep, both are measured, and both change what the paper should say. They matter more than the new findings.

Correction A

The mechanism claim is right; the seed-averaged table is not

An earlier version of this page argued that the draft's stated mechanism was wrong for the Adult MLP. That argument was itself wrong. It is retracted here, and the retraction is the more useful finding.

The seed-averaged row for Adult MLP at 90% sparsity reads accuracy 0.4980, balanced accuracy 0.5049, positive rate 0.5107. Read as a typical run, that says the model became a coin flip rather than a constant. The per-seed rows say otherwise.

accuracy0.2390.3350.2390.7610.6460.7610.2440.2390.7550.761

The distribution is bimodal, not central. Eight of ten seeds sit at or within one percent of a constant predictor: three collapse to always-positive (accuracy 0.2393, exactly the positive-class prevalence) and four to always-negative (accuracy 0.7607, exactly the majority-class rate). The mean of a set of ones and zeros is 0.5. That is all the 0.5107 was. Balanced accuracy could not disambiguate either, since a constant predictor scores exactly 0.5 and so does a coin flip.

The draft's mechanism is correct and should stand. The Adult MLP does degrade toward a near-constant predictor. What varies is which constant, by seed.

The finding that replaces the false one: at the collapsed end of the ladder, mean plus or minus standard deviation over seeds describes a model no seed actually produced. The paper reports these cells that way and calls the intermediate sparsities “seed-noisy”. The cause is not noise, it is bimodality, and the summary statistic is the wrong one. Report per seed, or report the modal outcome and the split.

Caught only because run_multiseed.py was changed to retain per-seed rows for the paired analysis. Without that change the aggregate would have been the only available view and the false claim would have survived.

Correction B

The headline model is the wrong one to headline

The crux question was: at what sparsity does the illusion appear, and would anyone deploy there? Measured against the majority-class baseline as the yardstick.

compas / logreg / *0.54500.5450no→ 0.000
compas / mlp / *0.49670.5450no→ 0.004

The spectacular 12× number comes from a model less accurate than always guessing the majority class. A reviewer will notice. The right move is to concede it: nobody ships that model, and any accuracy check catches it.

Stated per seed, so it does not rest on an average and cannot fall to the mistake in Correction A: across all ten seeds the Adult MLP at 90% sparsity reaches a maximum accuracy of 0.7607, which equals the majority-class baseline and never exceeds it. Not “the mean is below the trivial baseline” but “no individual run beats it”.

The Adult logistic cell is the one that matters. It gives up 2.7 points of accuracy, stays well above the trivial baseline, remains a model anyone would deploy, and still shows parity improving 28% (sex) to 41% (race) for no fairness reason. On sex its per-group calibration disparity simultaneously gets 2.7× worse. That is a real, deployable, silent illusion, and it is the honest headline.

What the control found

Rather than matching a single accuracy point, the control traces whole curves through accuracy space under three different ways of breaking the same model: magnitude pruning, training-label noise, and training subsampling.

Adult by race is the clean refutation. Pruning drives parity down; label noise drives it up. Same model, same data, comparable accuracy cost, opposite sign. Whatever pruning does to parity, generic damage does not do it.

The controls cannot even reach the collapse regime. Subsampling Adult from 36,631 training rows to about 730 costs two points of accuracy and raises parity. Flipping 40% of labels costs under three. Only weight-space damage pushes the model far enough. For these models, data-side damage degrades gracefully while weight-side damage destroys the decision function.

Scope narrowed

Damage mechanisms leave opposite signatures, in logistic regression only

Read the two Δ columns above as a pair. Pruning is a parity-collapse signature (Δparity −1.0 to −4.2, ΔECE +0.03 to +0.72). Label noise is a calibration-collapse signature (ΔECE +1.7 to +9.9, five to thirty times pruning's rate, with Δparity inconsistent in sign). Subsampling is neither.

This was written up from the logistic arm before the MLP arm finished. The MLP arm does not reproduce it. Per-seed median slopes, which is the robust statistic given the bimodality in Correction A:

Adult MLP, sexmedian ΔparityIQRmedian ΔECE
prune−0.3170.2950.617

The ordering inverts: on the MLP, pruning damages calibration three times faster than label noise, the opposite of the logistic result, and the parity slopes overlap once the interquartile ranges are counted. The honest claim is scoped to logistic regression on both datasets. Still a diagnostic worth reporting, still an argument for the on-device framing, but as a linear-model result rather than a universal one.

Confirmed

Pruning destabilises the MLP, and only the MLP

Replacing the generality the finding above lost, the MLP arm produced a cleaner discriminator of its own. Standard deviation of the positive prediction rate across seeds, at each mechanism's most extreme setting, on Adult.

modelprunelabel noisesubsampleratio
logreg0.0080.0300.0140.3×
mlp0.4610.0170.01626.9×

On the MLP, pruning produces roughly 27 times the run-to-run variance of either control, spanning the entire interval: some seeds collapse to always-positive, others to always-negative. On logistic regression pruning is more stable than the controls.

Both architectures end at a near-constant predictor with parity at zero, and that is the robust common claim. How they get there differs. Logistic regression walks there smoothly and reproducibly, which is why the signature separation works. The MLP jumps there unpredictably, which is why it does not. Neither discriminator generalises across both, and the paper should say which architecture each holds for.

The pruned model is not approaching the constant predictor. It matches it on every column to six decimal places.

This overturns the mitigation currently on the table. Equalized odds is not harder to fool at the collapsed endpoint. A constant predictor has a true-positive rate of 0 and a false-positive rate of 0 in every group, so both gaps are 0 and equalized odds reads exactly 0.0. Adding it to the report does not protect the audit. Only calibration catches this: over the same collapse, per-group ECE disparity rose from 0.039 to 0.086.

Robustness and statistics

Confirmed

The calibration blow-up survives adaptive binning

One fitted ensemble per cell, scored four ways, so the comparison is exactly paired. Ratio of mean per-group ECE at 90% sparsity to uncompressed.

The headline holds: the ~10× blow-up on the Adult MLP is stable to within 4% across all four estimators, so it is not a binning artifact.

  • Placement matters more than count. Changing 10→15 bins moves ECE by at most 4%. Changing equal-width→equal-mass moves absolute COMPAS numbers by up to 39%. The originally proposed 10-vs-15 check would have found nothing, though the instinct to test the estimator was right.
  • Distinct confidence values independently confirm Correction A. Uncompressed→90%: COMPAS logreg 570 → 31 → 1; Adult MLP 9541 → 2752. One becomes literally a single repeated confidence, which is why all four binnings agree there. The other keeps thousands of distinct values. Same parity collapse, demonstrably different mechanisms.

Going from 5 seeds to 10 changed this conclusion, which is a reminder not to settle a power question on half the data. At 5 seeds only the 90% rung survived. At 10 it extends the defensible claim down to 70% in four of six cells, while 30% and 50% stay firmly null everywhere. The paper can claim the 70–90% range and state plainly that 30% and 50% were tested and show nothing.

Also: int8 quantization is an exact no-op — paired difference 0.000000, p = 1.0. “Two compression methods” is really one method plus a null, and should be reported as such.

Negative

The collapse does not reduce to a single law

Tested and did not hold. Regressing the change in parity on the change in positive prediction rate, pooled over all 360 logistic rows: slope 0.87, R² = 0.30. Under pruning alone R² = 0.44. The hypothesis was that the apparent fairness gain is a deterministic readout of collapse, which would have been a tidy claim. It is a real correlation but not a tight one, and the per-mechanism slopes disagree in sign. Reporting it as a law would be overclaiming.

What this changes for the paper

  • Lead with Adult logistic regression, not the Adult MLP. Smaller headline number, real claim.
  • Keep the near-constant-predictor mechanism. It is correct, and it is now measured rather than asserted.
  • Stop summarising the collapsed cells with a mean. They are bimodal; the large standard deviations at high sparsity are bimodality, not noise.
  • The causal framing survives, so a title asserting compression-induced effects is defensible on the logistic arm.
  • Scope the signature finding to logistic regression. It inverts on the MLP, and reporting it as universal would not survive review.
  • Report the architecture split. Both families end at a near-constant predictor; logistic regression walks there reproducibly, the MLP jumps there with 27× the run-to-run variance.
  • Drop the “report equalized odds as well” mitigation. It is wrong.
  • Claim the 70–90% range, and say 30% and 50% were tested and show nothing.
  • State the calibration result as tested, not assumed.
  • Report int8 as a null result rather than a second method.

Not done, and worth naming as future work rather than quietly omitting: the ACSIncome compression arm, Brier and NLL, reliability diagrams, a third compression method, small-group instability, and per-group recall breakdowns. The paper text itself has not been edited — every change above lives in code, data, and figures.

Reproducibility check worth recording: the regenerated sweep matches the previous one to exactly zero difference on all 18 shared metric columns across all 52 previously existing cells. The new pipeline stage adds columns without perturbing any existing number, so the paper's current tables remain valid. Only peak RAM moves, by a few bytes in both directions on measurements of 63 KB to 13 MB, which is allocator jitter sampled before the new stage runs.
