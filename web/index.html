On-device model auditing: what we measured

A model can pass a fairness check and still be unfair. You just have to look at its uncertainty.

TinyAudit audits small, compressed models for fairness, uncertainty, and explainability together, inside the same memory budget the model runs in. Two findings, one of them replicated on a second dataset, each read off a tracked, regenerable result file.

Finding 01 · Adult · logistic model

The fairness lenses disagree about who is worst served

Point-prediction fairness and calibration fairness rank the groups in a different order. Ranked by how often each racial group is selected (the usual fairness view), then by calibration error (how much the model's confidence can be trusted), the lines cross. White is the best-calibrated group by a wide margin yet one of the most-selected, and the smaller groups carry higher, noisier calibration error that the selection view never surfaces. A demographic-parity-only audit sees none of this. Values are means over 10 seeds.

Finding 01 · replication · COMPAS · logistic model

The same disagreement shows up on a second, higher-stakes dataset

On COMPAS criminal-risk scoring, the point-fairness lens and the calibration lens still rank the racial groups differently. Here the "selected" outcome is being flagged high-risk, so a higher rate is worse for the group. African-American defendants are flagged at nearly twice the rate of the next group (0.44 vs 0.25, the well-known COMPAS bias), yet they are not the group whose confidence is least reliable: the worst-calibrated group is the one flagged least. Point parity and calibration parity point at different groups. Values are means over 10 seeds.

Finding 02 · Adult · neural net · pruning sweep

Compression can hide unfairness

From uncompressed to heavily pruned, it looks fairer on demographic parity while its calibration collapses. Both are error-like quantities on the same 0 to 0.40 scale, so they share one axis. The parity gap falls toward zero, not because the model became fair, but because it degraded into a near-constant predictor that treats everyone the same. A point-fairness audit would sign that off. Lines are 10-seed means; the shaded bands are ±1 std, so read the endpoints, not the wiggle between them.

Read honestly · caveats

What each number does and doesn't mean

  • scalingFeatures are standardized first. Mixed-range columns (0/1 flags vs. tens-of-thousands) make the neural net unusable; scaling fixes it and also makes models sturdier under compression.
  • dataAll three datasets are real. Adult and COMPAS load offline; Folktables is real 2018 California Census data (ACSIncome, ~196k rows) via the folktables package, falling back to a clearly-warned synthetic frame only when both the package and network are missing. Trees have no weights to prune, so they sit out the compression grid.
  • int8Quantized models show blank uncertainty. Their weights are locked in a format we can't peek inside to build the estimate, so we leave it blank rather than fake it.
  • compasHeavily-pruned COMPAS is degenerate. With only ~5 features it collapses to “predict the same for everyone,” which is trivially fair and meaningless.
  • methodThe uncertainty ensemble is 5 jittered copies of the one compressed model, not 5 from-scratch retrains, so read magnitudes as relative. Exact reproducibility is Linux-only.
